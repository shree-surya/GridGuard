GridGuard

Your appliance's bodyguard.

A plug-in protector for any appliance up to 6A. You set the voltage and current limits on a web page it serves from its own WiFi. If the supply or the load goes outside them, it sounds an alarm and cuts the power. Below is the same logic running in your browser: drag the supply and load, then watch it decide.

How it behaves

Event log

Every trip and reconnect is written down with its reason and the readings at that moment. On the real unit the log lives in flash memory, so it survives a power cut. Times come from your phone when you open the page.

How a trip works

The unit checks the readings several times a second. A short spike should not cut the power, so it waits out the trip delay before it acts.

  1. ArmedRelay closed. All four limits are checked continuously.
  2. TrippingA limit is crossed. The trip delay counts down. If the reading recovers, it goes back to armed.
  3. TrippedRelay opens, buzzer sounds, red LED lights, and the reason is logged.
  4. WaitingAutomatic mode: once the supply is normal, the restart delay counts down. Manual mode: it waits for a reset.
  5. Armed againRelay closes. If the fault is still there, it trips again.
SettingWhat it does
Over-voltage and under-voltageTrip when the supply goes above or below your limits.
Over-currentTrip when the appliance draws more than your limit. Keep it at or below 7A, under the relay's rating.
Under-currentTrip when the draw falls too low, such as a pump running dry. Not below about 0.3A, where the sensor gets noisy.
Trip delayHow long a fault must last before the relay opens.
HysteresisA small margin so readings hovering at a limit do not make the relay chatter.
Restart delayWait before reconnecting. Fridges need about three minutes.
After a tripReconnect on its own, or wait for a reset from the touch pad, the red button or the web page.

One detail the demo copies from the real unit: with the relay open it cannot see the load current. After a current trip it simply retries when the restart delay ends, and trips again if the fault is still there.

The build

A 6A junction-style box in a tinted case. It looks like a finished product: status lights, an alarm and a red reset button, with the wiring tucked behind the smoked cover.

  • Back: 6A plug to the wall, on a short cord.
  • Front: 6A socket, 1.3 inch OLED, status LEDs, buzzer, touch pad, red reset button and the MCB.
  • Smoked cover: tinted polycarbonate with tool-only screws, over insulated terminal covers.
  • Relay: wired so it is open when unpowered. A dead controller means the load is off.
  • Two zones: mains parts on one side and the low-voltage ESP32 side on the other, with a barrier between them.
  • Power for the ESP32: a sealed 5 V Hi-Link module with a 1 A slow-blow fuse, taken from before the relay.
  • Settings: kept in flash, so limits survive a power cut.

The MCB is the real protection. It stops short circuits in milliseconds and does not depend on software. GridGuard's relay is a second layer for voltage and current limits, and it is rated for loads up to 6A.

GridGuard. A learning and demonstration project on 230 V mains. Not a certified protection device. Handle with care.
